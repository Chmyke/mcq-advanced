# THEME french-grammar | French grammar
COURSE: French, unit 3, tenses and moods

## Q passe-compose | level 2 | 30s
Which form completes the sentence « Hier, nous ___ au cinéma. »?

- [ ] avons allé
  > « Aller » forms its passé composé with « être », not « avoir ».
- [x] sommes allés
  > The passé composé tells a finished past action; « aller » takes « être ».
- [ ] irons
  > The future contradicts « hier ».

RULE: A finished action at a given past time takes the passé composé.
REVIEW: French, unit 3, passé composé

## Q translation-for | level 3 | 30s
Which sentence translates "I have lived in Lyon for three years"?

- [x] J'habite à Lyon depuis trois ans.
  > The action began in the past and goes on: French uses the present with « depuis ».
- [ ] J'ai habité à Lyon pour trois ans.
  > A word-for-word copy of the English: « pour » does not give a duration up to now.
- [ ] J'habite à Lyon pendant trois ans.
  > « Pendant » gives a duration with no link to the present moment.

RULE: An action that started in the past and still goes on: present tense + « depuis ».
REVIEW: French, unit 3, depuis

## Q unreal-condition | level 4 | 40s
Which word completes « Si je ___ riche, je ferais le tour du monde. »?

- [ ] serais
  > The conditional goes in the main clause, never right after « si ».
- [ ] serai
  > The future would need « je ferai » in the main clause, and it never follows « si » either.
- [x] étais
  > An imagined condition: « si » + imparfait, then the conditional.

RULE: Unreal condition: « si » + imparfait, then the conditional in the main clause.
REVIEW: French, unit 3, the conditional

## Q false-friends | level 3 | 30s
Which of these French words are false friends, with a meaning different from the English word they look like?

- [x] librairie
  > It means "bookshop"; a library is « une bibliothèque ».
- [ ] important
  > Same meaning as in English.
- [x] actuellement
  > It means "currently", not "actually" (« en fait »).
- [ ] information
  > Same meaning as in English.

RULE: A word that looks like English can mean something else: check it in context.
REVIEW: French, unit 3, vocabulary

# THEME reading | Reading
COURSE: French, unit 5, reading comprehension

## Q j-ai-hate | level 1 | 20s
What does « J'ai hâte » mean?

- [ ] I hate it
  > « Hâte » looks like "hate", but it means eagerness.
- [x] I can't wait
  > « Avoir hâte » means looking forward to something.
- [ ] I am late
  > Being late is « être en retard ».

RULE: Look past the shape of a word to its meaning in the phrase.
REVIEW: French, unit 5, common expressions

## Q tom-late | level 3 | 40s
Read: « Tom a raté le bus, alors il est allé à l'école à pied et il est arrivé en retard. » Why was Tom late?

- [x] He missed his bus
  > « A raté le bus »: he missed it, then had to walk.
- [ ] The bus was late
  > The text speaks of Tom being late, never of the bus.
- [ ] He woke up late
  > The text says nothing about when he woke up.

RULE: Base every answer on a precise word of the text.
REVIEW: French, unit 5, finding a cause

## Q subjunctive | level 4 | 40s
In « Il faut que tu viennes », why is the form « viennes » used?

- [ ] It is the future
  > The future would be « tu viendras ».
- [ ] It is the imperfect
  > The imperfect would be « tu venais ».
- [x] It is the subjunctive
  > « Il faut que » expresses a necessity and is followed by the subjunctive.

RULE: « Il faut que » is always followed by the subjunctive.
REVIEW: French, unit 5, the subjunctive

## Q lower-case-days | level 2 | 20s
True or false? In French, the names of days and months are written in lower case.

- [x] True
  > One writes « lundi » and « janvier », unlike English.
- [ ] False
  > It is English that writes "Monday" and "January" with a capital.

RULE: In French, days, months and languages take a small letter.
REVIEW: French, unit 5, spelling
