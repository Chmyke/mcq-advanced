# THEME grammar | Grammar and spelling
COURSE: English, chapter 2, grammar

## Q apostrophe-its | level 2 | 30s
Which sentence uses "its" or "it's" correctly?

- [ ] The dog wagged it's tail.
  > "It's" always stands for "it is" or "it has".
- [x] The dog wagged its tail.
  > "Its" is the possessive, like "his" or "her": no apostrophe.
- [ ] The dog wagged its' tail.
  > "Its'" is no English word at all.

RULE: "It's" is a contraction; "its" is a possessive and takes no apostrophe.
REVIEW: English, chapter 2, apostrophes

## Q word-function | level 3 | 30s
In "She sings beautifully", what is the function of "beautifully"?

- [x] An adverb modifying the verb
  > It tells how she sings, so it modifies "sings".
- [ ] An adjective describing the subject
  > An adjective would describe "she", as in "She is beautiful".
- [ ] The object of the verb
  > An object answers "what?": she sings a song.

RULE: An adverb of manner tells how an action is done and modifies the verb.
REVIEW: English, chapter 2, parts of speech

## Q verb-tense | level 3 | 40s
Which tense is used in "By noon, they will have finished the work"?

- [ ] The present perfect
  > The present perfect would be "they have finished".
- [ ] The past perfect
  > The past perfect would be "they had finished".
- [x] The future perfect
  > "Will have" plus the past participle: finished before a point in the future.

RULE: A perfect tense is named after the tense of its auxiliary "have".
REVIEW: English, chapter 2, perfect tenses

## Q correct-sentences | level 4 | 40s
Which of these sentences are grammatically correct in formal English?

- [x] Neither of the answers is right.
  > "Neither" is singular: it takes "is".
- [ ] Between you and I, the plan is weak.
  > After a preposition, the object pronoun is needed: "between you and me".
- [x] Fewer people came than last year.
  > "Fewer" goes with things you can count, such as people.
- [ ] Less students passed this year.
  > Students can be counted: "fewer students".

RULE: Check what a word agrees with or refers to before judging the sentence.
REVIEW: English, chapter 2, agreement and usage

# THEME literature | Literature
COURSE: English, chapter 4, literature

## Q author-pride | level 1 | 20s
Who wrote the novel "Pride and Prejudice", published in 1813?

- [ ] Charlotte Brontë
  > Charlotte Brontë wrote "Jane Eyre", published in 1847.
- [x] Jane Austen
  > Austen published it in 1813, two years after "Sense and Sensibility".
- [ ] George Eliot
  > George Eliot wrote "Middlemarch".

RULE: Link each major work to its author and its century.
REVIEW: English, chapter 4, the nineteenth-century novel

## Q figure-of-speech | level 3 | 40s
"Parting is such sweet sorrow" (Shakespeare, Romeo and Juliet): which figure of speech does it use?

- [x] An oxymoron
  > "Sweet" and "sorrow" pull in opposite directions and are joined in one phrase.
- [ ] A hyperbole
  > A hyperbole exaggerates, which this line does not do.
- [ ] A simile
  > A simile compares with "like" or "as".

RULE: An oxymoron joins two words of opposite meaning in one expression.
REVIEW: English, chapter 4, figures of speech

## Q poem-form | level 3 | 40s
A poem has fourteen lines: three quatrains and a final couplet, rhymed ABAB CDCD EFEF GG. What is its form?

- [ ] A ballad
  > A ballad tells a story in many short stanzas, often with a refrain.
- [ ] A limerick
  > A limerick has five lines, rhymed AABBA.
- [x] A sonnet
  > Fourteen lines in three quatrains and a couplet: the Shakespearean sonnet.

RULE: The Shakespearean sonnet has three quatrains and a closing couplet.
REVIEW: English, chapter 4, poetic forms

## Q narrator-character | level 2 | 20s
True or false? In a first-person narrative, the narrator is a character in the story.

- [x] True
  > The narrator says "I" because they take part in what they tell.
- [ ] False
  > A narrator outside the story tells it in the third person.

RULE: The pronoun of the narrative shows whether the narrator takes part in the story.
REVIEW: English, chapter 4, point of view
